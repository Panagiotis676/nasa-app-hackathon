Earth Jukebox

Hear a map of our planet. Each Earth science map frame is turned into live sound, so changes in the data can be heard, not just seen. Everything works with a keyboard and a screen reader.

Data

The night lights map is real NASA data and is ready to play. To hear other data, export map frames from the NASA Earth Information Center and add them here in time order (files are sorted by name). Then choose the colormap (or upload a picture of the map’s colorbar) and the value range printed on it.

Crop that picture so only the coloured bar is in it. A wide bar is read from left (minimum) to right (maximum), a tall bar from bottom (minimum) to top (maximum).

Advanced: colour tolerance, map area and map extent

If your images have a title, a colorbar or a border around the map, trim it off so only the map itself is used.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Space play or pause
  • Right arrow or N next frame
  • Left arrow or P previous frame
  • R replay the frame
  • D describe the frame
  • L how to listen

How it works

  • Colour to number. Pixel colours are matched back to data values using the colormap you choose, or your colorbar image. For maps without a colormap, like night lights, brightness is the value.
  • Left to right. The sound sweeps west to east, with your left ear at the west edge and your right ear at the east edge.
  • Pitch and loudness. Pitch is latitude, with north higher and south lower, and loudness is the data value. Pitches use a pentatonic scale so any data sounds musical, and silence means no data.
  • Change over time. Values are scaled the same way across all frames, so changes over time stay audible. A rising chime between frames means the average went up, and a falling chime means it went down.

Colours decoded from an image are an approximation of the true data.
